I'm not sure whether bagging [botanical gardens](http://www.bgci.org/garden_search.php "BCGI botanic garden search") is better or worse than bagging Munros, Michelin stars or the numbers off of rolling stock, but it keeps me off the streets...

## London (Kew)

Just ten stops down the District Line from \$WORK lies the [Royal Botanic Gardens Kew](http://www.kew.org/ "Royal Botanic Gardens Kew"). The gardens have three enormous glasshouses, a number of smaller glasshouses, and 121 hectares of trees, beds and [desperately awful architecture](http://www.kew.org/visit-kew-gardens/explore/attractions/pagoda "Kew Pagoda") to explore. Unfortunately, it also has an entry fee (for non-concession adults) of £14.50, which is a little steep, and possibly one of the reasons that disappointingly few of my students seem to have visited it, despite its proximity.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:199px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/kew_gardens_temperate_house.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/kew_gardens_temperate_house-199x300.jpg" alt="Kew gardens temperate house [CC-BY-2.0 Alex Lomas]" /></a><figcaption>Temperate House (closed for renovations at the time of writing)</figcaption></figure>

My favourite indoor displays at Kew are the two rooms of carnivorous plants in the Princess of Wales Conservatory (don't miss the newer cloud-forest full of *Nepenthes*), and the ever-changing contents of the Alpine and Waterlily Houses. The latter often has large clumps of sensitive plants (*Mimosa pudica* and relatives) to poke. I also enjoy the very Victorian approach to health-and-safety in the walkway at the top of the Palm House.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:224px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/kew_gardens_nepenthes.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/kew_gardens_nepenthes-224x300.jpg" alt="Kew gardens Nepenthes [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption><em>Nepenthes robcantelyi</em></figcaption></figure>

Botanerd highlights.  

1.  Play "palm or [cycad](/2012/04/why-living-fossils-need-to-die/ "Why living fossils need to die")?" at the two ends of the Palm House.
2.  Flowers are essentially tarts. Prostitutes for the bees. The Princess of Wales Conservatory has a very good selection of huge flower-free ferns and spikemosses (*Selaginella*).
3.  There's a gigantic *Ginkgo* just round the back of the Princess of Wales Conservatory; and the oft ignored (and therefore much less busy) far end of the gardens has a fantastic collection of conifers, including *Araucaria*, *Sequoia*, [*Sequoiadendron*](/2013/08/organism-of-the-week-16/ "Organism of the week #16 – From tiny acorns do mighty oaks grow"), *Torreya*, *Cunninghamia*, and *Cryptomeria,* in addition to all the yews, cypresses, pines, firs, larches, cedars and spruces you can eat.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:199px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/kew_araucaria.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/kew_araucaria-199x300.jpg" alt="Kew gardens Araucaria [CC-BY-2.0 Alex Lomas]" /></a><figcaption>Monkey-puzzle (<em>Araucaria araucana</em>) at Kew</figcaption></figure>

London (Chelsea)

Small but perfectly formed, the [Chelsea Physic Garden](http://chelseaphysicgarden.co.uk/ "Chelsea Physic Garden") is one of the oldest botanic gardens in the world (Oxford, below, claims the top spot). It specialises in plants used by humans, including (when I last went) a special display of plant fibre ropes. Entry about £10.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:300px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/chelsea_physic_garden_ropes.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/chelsea_physic_garden_ropes-300x234.jpg" alt="Chelsea physic garden ropes [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption>Hemp rope smells nicest and can be drawn very fast over skin without causing friction burns, which is an important consideration for, erm, rigging - yes - rigging</figcaption></figure>

Botanerd highlights.  

1.  Count the number of exciting ways you could get killed by the plants in the Pharmaceutical Garden display.

  

## Edinburgh

  
I don't remember the [Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh](http://www.rbge.org.uk/ "Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh") being this sunny either time we visited, but apparently it was on at least one trip. Unlike Kew, the glasshouses are squidged together, so if the weather's misbehaving, your fern to rain ratio will be much higher than in London. Unfortunately, *like* Kew, at the time of writing, some of the glasshouses are shut for renovations. Console yourself with the fact that entry to the gardens themselves is free.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:300px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/edinburgh_botanical_gardens.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/edinburgh_botanical_gardens-300x225.jpg" alt="Edinburgh botanical gardens [CC-BY-2.0 Alex Lomas]" /></a><figcaption>Edinburgh botanical gardens</figcaption></figure>

Botanerd highlights.  

1.  Edinburgh is the only place I've ever seen a clubmoss (*Lycopodium*) on display. See if it's still there.
2.  It's also the only place I've ever seen a *Gnetum* (picture at the end of this post).

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:242px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/edinburgh_lycopodium.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/edinburgh_lycopodium-242x300.jpg" alt="Edinburgh botanical gardens Lycopodium [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption><em>Lycopodium pinifolia</em> at Edinburgh</figcaption></figure>

## Barcelona

Sitting on the slopes of Montjuïc, just below the Olympic Stadium, the [Jardí Botànic de Barcelona](www.jardibotanic.bcn.es "Jardí Botànic de Barcelona") is my most recent bagging. Unlike the other gardens here, it is entirely outdoors, with no glasshouses, and therefore specialises in plants from [Mediterranean scrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_forests,_woodlands,_and_scrub "Mediterranean vegetation at Wikipedia") habitats like Chile, South West Australia and California. Entry fee to the gardens is a very reasonable €3.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:224px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/barcelona_xanthorrhoea.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/barcelona_xanthorrhoea-224x300.jpg" alt="Barcelona botanical gardens Xanthorrhoea [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption>Grass-trees (<em>Xanthorrhoea</em>) at the Barcelona botanical gardens</figcaption></figure>

Botanerd highlights.  

1.  Australian grass-trees and giant Chilean [*Puya* bromeliads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puya_chilensis "Puya chilensis at Wikipedia").
2.  As you're on the side of a hill, the views are also fantastic, and the easiest way to get there is [via cable-car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montju%C3%AFc_Cable_Car "Montjuïc Cable Car at Wikipedia"), so you get to soar over the local conifers too.

  

## Amsterdam

  
The photo below of [De Hortus Botanicus Amsterdam](http://dehortus.nl/en/home "De Hortus Botanicus Amsterdam") doesn't do it justice, but it's well worth the €8.50 entry. The glasshouses are very well laid out, and they have a very good selection of carnivorous plants, obscure ferns (including *Marattia*) and [cycads](/2012/04/why-living-fossils-need-to-die/ "Why living fossils need to die").

## Oxford

Claiming to be the oldest botanic garden in the world (and I've no reason to doubt them!), the [University of Oxford Botanic Garden](http://www.botanic-garden.ox.ac.uk/ "University of Oxford Botanic Garden") is a snip at £4.50 entry, and has a good mixture of outdoor beds and glasshouses. The glasshouses are small, but absolutely rammed with stuff, including *Pachypodium* (below), assorted ferns, jade vines, a lovely [*Amorphophallus*](/2013/03/organism-of-the-week-10/ "Organism of the week #10 – The shapeless cock-of-the-gods") *rivieri* (well, lovely until you stick your nose over it), but - as it turns out - no [*Orchis fatalis*](http://midsomermurders.org/oxfordloc.htm "Midsomer Murders").

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:224px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/oxford_botanical_gardens_pachypodium.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/oxford_botanical_gardens_pachypodium-224x300.jpg" alt="Oxford botanical gardens Pachypodium [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption>Oxford botanical gardens <em>Pachypodium</em></figcaption></figure>

Botanerd highlights.  

1.  This is the only place I've ever seen a [*Psilotum*](/2013/04/organism-of-the-week-11/ "Organism of the week #11 – Not even primitive"), which had me squealing with excitement, much to the disdain and bafflement of my [long-suffering companion](http://www.alexlomas.com "Alex's blog") on these trips.
2.  Like the rest of this garden, the carnivorous plant glasshouse crams a lot of variety into a small space.

  

## Berlin

  
The [Botanischer Garten und Botanisches Museum Berlin-Dahlem](http://www.bgbm.org/ "Botanischer Garten und Botanisches Museum Berlin-Dahlem") claims to be the second-largest in the world (after Kew), and now has a [dedicated moss garden](http://www.bgbm.org/en/map-garden/moss-garden "Moss garden at Berlin Botanic Gardens") (which unfortunately post-dates my visit) as well as the usual beds and (extensive) glasshouses. Entry fee is €6. [UPDATE - I have now seen the moss garden with my own eyes and it makes me weep with happiness](/2018/01/moosgartenruckreisefreude/).

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:225px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/berlin_botanical_gardens.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/berlin_botanical_gardens-225x300.jpg" alt="Berlin botanical gardens [CC-BY-2.0 Alex Lomas]" /></a><figcaption>Berlin botanical gardens</figcaption></figure>

Botanerd highlights.  

1.  Several of the botanic gardens mentioned above cultivate [*Welwitschia mirabilis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welwitschia "Welwitschia at Wikipedia"), a very strange plant from the Namib that grows only two enormous strap-like leaves in its lifetime, but only Berlin seems to have been completely successful: their plants are verdant and frequently in flower ('in cone', really, as [this plant is closely related to the pines and other conifers](/2013/03/vegetable-empire-2013/ "Vegetable empire 2013")).

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:260px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/berlin_welwitschia.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/berlin_welwitschia-260x300.jpg" alt="Berlin botanical gardens Welwitschia [CC-BY-2.0 Alex Lomas]" /></a><figcaption><em>Welwitschia mirabilis</em> at Berlin</figcaption></figure>

## (Dis)honourable mentions

I didn't quite make it into the <a href="http://www.sfbotanicalgarden.org/" style="font-size: 12px;" title="San Francisco Botanical Garden">San Francisco Botanical Garden</a>, but perhaps one day I'll return with more time, and having not been recently fleeced at the <a href="http://www.calacademy.org/" style="font-size: 12px;" title="California Academy of Sciences">California Academy of Sciences</a> (\$30 entry!)

Brussels has a wholly confusing pair of botanic gardens, the [National Botanic Garden of Belgium](http://www.br.fgov.be/ "National Botanic Garden of Belgium"), which is just north of Brussels, and the [Botanical Garden of Brussels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_Garden_of_Brussels "Botanical Garden of Brussels at Wikipedia"), which sits on the real botanic garden's *old* site in the middle of Brussels. I got the former mixed up with the latter, much to my disappointment. It's perfectly pleasant, but not really a botanic garden.

[Darwin's House at Down in Kent](/2013/04/down-amongst-the-butterworts/ "Down amongst the butterworts") has a small glasshouse with a good collection of carnivorous plants. Well worth a visit, and a wander down the sandwalk.

## Where next?

In particular, I'd love to know where I can see the following [obscure corners of the vegetable empire](/2013/03/vegetable-empire-2013/ "Vegetable empire 2013"):

- *Ophioglossum* or *Botrychium* (adders-tongue fern).
- Hornworts (*Anthoceros*), and/or a really good moss and liverwort display (preferably closer than Berlin!)
- Quillworts (*Isoetes*).
- *[Amborella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amborella "Amborella at Wikipedia").* [UPDATE - spotted at Berlin!](/2018/01/moosgartenruckreisefreude/)
- *Utricularia tenella* or *Utricularia multifida* (previously *Polypompholyx tenella* and *Polypompholyx multifida*, until Peter Taylor cast the fairy aprons into the eternal darkness of taxonomic obsolescence).
