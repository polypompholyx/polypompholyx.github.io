The [Barbican](https://www.barbican.org.uk/) is a sprawling concrete brutality as apt to divide opinion as Marmite or skinny jeans. Built in the 60s and 70s as a housing estate and cultural centre on a vast bomb-site in the City of London, it does not sound like a promising place to go botanising, but after the [fire-weed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamaenerion_angustifolium) was concreted over, other plants moved in...

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:225px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Erigeron_karvinskianus.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Erigeron_karvinskianus-e1546430166618-225x300.jpg" alt="Erigeron karvinskianus [CC-BY-SA-4.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption>Fleabane (<em>Erigeron karvinskianus</em>) growing on a bastion that formed part of the original <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Wall">London Wall</a></figcaption></figure>

Aside from the fleabane gussying up the scheduled monuments, and the friendly rivalry of the residents' window-boxes, the Barbican also houses a [large conservatory](https://www.barbican.org.uk/whats-on/tag/conservatory) containing more than 2000 species of plant. It is open to the public most Sundays.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:300px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Barbican_glasshouse.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Barbican_glasshouse-e1546431185903-300x225.jpg" alt="Barbican glasshouse [CC-BY-SA-4.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption>Barbican Conservatory: main glasshouse</figcaption></figure>

A highlight of the conservatory complex is the arid-zone glasshouse, which crams a surprising variety of cactuses and other succulents into quite a small space. Like many botanerds, my sickness started in childhood, when I broke out in a rash of succulents and carnivorous plants - as I enter my dotage, nostalgia grips me and I am drawn back to these plants like a homeopath to idiocy.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:300px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Barbican_arid_zone.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Barbican_arid_zone-e1546432042893-300x225.jpg" alt="Barbican arid zone glasshouse [CC-BY-SA-4.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption>Barbican arid zone glasshouse</figcaption></figure>

Is this a botanic garden? Can it be included in my baggings? As far as I'm concerned, the key difference between a garden and a *botanic* garden is whether the plants have merely been plonked there to be gawped at, or whether the plants are properly labelled with Linnaean binomials and can thus be ticked off a list as God intended:

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:225px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Lophophora_williamsii_Barbican.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Lophophora_williamsii_Barbican-e1546431378449-225x300.jpg" alt="Lophophora williamsii [CC-BY-SA-4.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption>Labels: check. <em>Lophophora williamsii</em></figcaption></figure>

The plants are labelled; Flora be praised; this *is* a botanic garden.
