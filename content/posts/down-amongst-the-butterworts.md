I lived within 30 minutes' drive of Charles Darwin's house for the whole of my childhood. It's been open to the public since it was acquired by English Heritage in 1998. I've been a biologist of sorts for about 18 years.

It's taken me until *today* to actually [visit Down House](http://www.english-heritage.org.uk/daysout/properties/home-of-charles-darwin-down-house/ "Down House at English Heritage"), and to wander down the famous sandwalk behind the house, where Darwin used to do his thinking. I am a very bad, and very lazy boy. Also a very lucky one to have a husband prepared to schlepp to [yet *another* greenhouse](/2013/04/organism-of-the-week-11/ "Organism of the week #11 – Not even primitive").

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:224px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/sandwalk.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/sandwalk-224x300.jpg" alt="The Sandwalk at Down House [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption>The Sandwalk at Down House</figcaption></figure>

Darwin's greatest work was - of course - his 1875 treatise [*Insectivorous Plants*](http://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5765 "Darwin's Insectivorous Plants at Project Gutenberg") in which he described the carnivorous activities of sundews, butterworts and their ilk.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:300px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/Drosera_rotundifolia.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/Drosera_rotundifolia-300x240.jpg" alt="Drosera rotundifolia [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption>Darwin did much of his observations of carnivory in plants on the <a title="Drosera rotundifolia at Wikipedia" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drosera_rotundifolia">round-leaved sundew <em>Drosera rotundifolia</em></a>. This is a photo of some of these plants at Darwin's glasshouse.</figcaption></figure>

The glasshouses at Down are home to a small but beautifully formed collection of plants including the lovely [large-flowered butterwort](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinguicula_grandiflora "Pinguicula grandiflora at WIkipedia"), *Pinguicula grandiflora*, one of just nine species of insectivorous plant native to Britain and Ireland (three sundews, three butterworts, three bladderworts, plus [one introduced pitcher-plant](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarracenia_purpurea "Purple pitcher plant at Wikipedia")).

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:224px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/Pinguicula_grandiflora.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/Pinguicula_grandiflora-224x300.jpg" alt="Pinguicula grandiflora [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption><em>Pinguicula grandiflora</em>, also at Down House</figcaption></figure>

This species forms a hibernaculum, a very tight overwintering bud, like a more extreme version of th[e non-carnivorous winter rosettes of my Mexican species](/2013/04/the-kraken-wakes/ "The kraken wakes"), so we were quite lucky to see them in flower given that it's been winter since - IIRC - September 2010.
