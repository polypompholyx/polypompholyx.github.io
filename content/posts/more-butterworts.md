In winter, a lot of butterwort species give up on making large, sticky, carnivorous leaves, and instead produce compact non-carnivorous rosettes that look a bit like houseleeks:

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:265px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/Pinguicula_cyclosecta_winter_rosette.jpg"><img loading="lazy" title="Pinguicula cyclosecta winter rosette [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Steve Cook]" alt="Pinguicula cyclosecta winter rosette [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Steve Cook]" src="/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/Pinguicula_cyclosecta_winter_rosette-265x300.jpg" /></a><figcaption><em>Pinguicula cyclosecta</em> winter rosette, remains of carnivorous summer rosette slowly composting away</figcaption></figure>

Carnivory is not free, so this is [probably an adaptation to the lack of insect prey](http://dx.doi.org/10.1086/284289 "Givnish, T. J., Burkhardt, R. E., Happel, R. E. & Weintraub, J. D. (1984) Carnivory in the bromeliad Brocchinia reducta with a cost/benefit model for the general restriction of carnivorous plants to sunny, moist and nutrient-poor habitats. The American Naturalist, 124, 479-497.") available during the winter months.

When I grow up I want [one of these](http://www.pinguicula.org/pages/pages_principales/Dripping_walls.htm "Dripping wall").
