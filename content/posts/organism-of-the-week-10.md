I missed last week, so this week's is a biggun, to make up for my tardiness.

Bow down before [The Shapeless Cock-of-the-Gods](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amorphophallus_titanum "Amorphophallus titanum at Wikipedia"):

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:225px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Amorphophallus_titanum_inflorescence.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Amorphophallus_titanum_inflorescence-225x300.jpg" alt="Amorphophallus titanum inflorescence [CC-BY-2.0 Alex Lomas]" /></a><figcaption><em>Amorphophallus titanum</em> inflorescence</figcaption></figure>

Apparently, when this plant was filmed for [*The Private Life of Plants*](http://www.bbc.co.uk/nature/life/Titan_arum#p004gx80 "Titan arum at the BBC") in 1995, David Attenborough decided to invent the term "titan arum" to avoid using the plant's Latin (well, Greek, really) name on prime-time BBC-1:

*Amorphophallus titanum*  

- ἄμορφος (*amorphos*) = without form, shapeless
- φαλλός (*phallos*) = phallus, penis, cock
- Τιτάν (*Titan*) = primaeval Greek god

  
Coming from Croydon, euphemism has never been my strong point. The titan arum will always be the shapeless cock-of-the-gods to me.

Interestingly, the closest British relative of the cock-of-the-gods is lords-and-ladies, otherwise known as the cuckoo-pint. The "pint" here does not refer to the unit of volume, but is a contraction of the Old English word *pintle*, which *also* means phallus, penis or cock. There's evidently something about the flowers of arum lilies that brings out the grubby little school-boy in taxonomists.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:273px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/arum_maculatum.jpg"><img loading="lazy" title="Arum maculatum [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Sannse@Wikipedia]" src="/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/arum_maculatum-273x300.jpg" alt="Arum maculatum [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Sannse@Wikipedia]" /></a><figcaption><em>Arum maculatum</em> [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Sannse@Wikipedia]</figcaption></figure>

The cock-of-the-gods is rather rare - possibly even endangered - in its native Sumatra, but Kew Gardens have a number of potted cocks in the Princess of Wales Conservatory. Its leaves are no less impressive than its inflorescence. The photo below might look like a small tree, but is in fact one massive, much-subdivided leaf.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:225px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Amorphophallus_titanum_leaf.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Amorphophallus_titanum_leaf-225x300.jpg" alt="Amorphophallus titanum leaf [CC-BY-2.0 Alex Lomas]" /></a><figcaption><em>Amorphophallus titanum</em> leaf</figcaption></figure>

The cock-of-the-gods often gets touted as the largest flower in the world. This is technically untrue: the largest flower in the word is that of the parasitic em\>Rafflesia:

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:225px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/rafflesia_arnoldii.jpg"><img loading="lazy" title="Rafflesia arnoldii [CC-BY-2.0 ma_suska@Wikipedia]" src="/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/rafflesia_arnoldii-225x300.jpg" alt="Rafflesia arnoldii [CC-BY-2.0 ma_suska@Wikipedia]" /></a><figcaption><em>Rafflesia arnoldii</em> [CC-BY-2.0 ma_suska@Wikipedia] Two things this plant shares with the cock-of-the-gods are its home (Sumatra) and its foul odour: both are pollinated by flies attracted by an odour mimicking rotting carrion</figcaption></figure>

What looks like the flower of the cock-of-the-gods is really a mass of tiny flowers on a central spike (the spadix) entirely surrounded by a large colourful leaf (the spathe). The same arrangement is found in most other [arum lilies](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Araceae "Aroids at Wikipedia"), including Swiss cheese plants, skunk cabbage, and peace lilies:

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:225px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/aroid.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/aroid-225x300.jpg" alt="Unidentified aroid [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption>Some arum lily or other. You can see the tiny unopened flowers on the surface of the spadix: they're packed together so tightly they are squished into hexagons.</figcaption></figure>

Kew's potted cocks are often in flower around May. The inflorescence doesn't last for long, and they don't get produced every year, so if [@KewGardens](https://twitter.com/kewgardens "Kew Gardens on Twitter") tweet that it's about to flower, you've got about a week to see it before it collapses into a rancid sack of squelch.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:225px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Amorphophallus_titanum_bud.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Amorphophallus_titanum_bud-225x300.jpg" alt="Amorphophallus titanum bud [CC-BY-2.0 Alex Lomas]" /></a><figcaption><em>Amorphophallus titanum</em> bud</figcaption></figure>

Strangely enough, the *smallest* flower in the world belongs to a relative of the cock-of-the-gods, a kind of duckweed called [*Wolffia*](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolffia "Wolffia at Wikipedia"):

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:300px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Wolffia_colombiana.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Wolffia_colombiana-300x225.jpg" alt="Wolffia colombiana [CC-BY-2.0 Alex Lomas]" /></a><figcaption><em>Wolffia colombiana</em>, each barely visible speck of green in the beaker is an entire duck-weed plant</figcaption></figure>

The ubiquity of duckweed and the rarity of the cock-of-the-gods is clear evidence that that it's not size that matters, but what you do with it.
