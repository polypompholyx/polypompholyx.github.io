Imperial's campus in Berkshire, [Silwood Park](http://www3.imperial.ac.uk/silwoodparkcampus "Silwood Park at Imperial College London"), is a fabulous place to go fungus spotting. The fly agaric (*Amanita muscaria*) is very common there as there are a lot of birch trees around, and this fungus forms a [symbiosis with the roots](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycorrhiza "Mycorrhiza at Wikipedia") of those trees:

[<img src="/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/Amanita_muscaria-225x300.jpg" class="aligncenter size-medium wp-image-598" width="225" height="300" alt="Amanita muscaria [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Steve Cook]" />](/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/Amanita_muscaria.jpg)Fly agarics are rather poisonous (for some value of 'rather'), by virtue of containing [muscimol](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscimol "Muscimol at Wikipedia"), and [ibotenic acid](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibotenic_acid "Ibotenic acid at Wikipedia"); the former is a psychoactive dissociative that can cause visual and auditory hallucinations. There is an enormous amount of folklore (*i.e.* lies) associated with this mushroom that I shan't bother repeating. There's not even much evidence that it's any use for killing flies.

Although fly agarics *are* toxic, they are nothing like as poisonous as their [amanitin](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha-Amanitin "Alpha amanitin at Wikipedia")-containing relatives, the cheerily named death cap ([*Amanita phalloides*](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanita_phalloides "Amanita phalloides at Wikipedia")) and the delightfully named destroying angel ([*Amanita virosa*](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanita_virosa "Amanita virosa at Wikipedia")), whose toxins don't merely bugger up your nerves, but poison every cell in your body at an [intimate molecular level](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_polymerase_II "RNA polymerase II at Wikipedia"). Fungi probably contain toxic compounds for the same reason as many plants and corals do: even if you can't bite or run away from your predators, you can still make them very, very sorry.
