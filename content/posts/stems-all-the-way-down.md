In the days when potted plants were more than just disposable land-filler, my Nan would try to force her wizened poinsettia to flower by sticking it into a bin-liner [with some apples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethylene#Ethylene_as_a_plant_hormone) every night from October until December. Sum total of flowers she ever saw: zero. Ho hum. But how many poinsettia flowers can *you* see in the picture below?

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:300px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/euphorbia_pulcherrima.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/euphorbia_pulcherrima-300x197.jpg" alt="Euphorbia pulcherrima [PD, USDA Scott Bauer]" /></a><figcaption>Poinsettia (<em>Euphorbia pulcherrima</em>)</figcaption></figure>

If you're wondering whether you can include the ones that are partly off-screen, you are asking the wrong question. There are many hundreds of flowers here, not six or ten. The red leafy-looking things you might have been cruelly mislead into thinking were petals are actually brightly coloured leaves. The flowers themselves are found in the tiny yellow-green blobs in the middle...

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:300px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/euphorbia_pulcherimma_cyathia.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/euphorbia_pulcherimma_cyathia-300x199.jpg" alt="Euphorbia pulcherimma cyathia [CC-BY-SA-2.5 André Karwath]" /></a><figcaption>Poinsettia cyathia [CC-BY-SA-2.5 André Karwath]</figcaption></figure>

...but they are not - as it turns out - the blobs themselves. Each one of those blobs is *itself* a bunch of tiny petal-less flowers: one petal-less female flower, and a bunch of tiny petal-less male flowers consisting of just one anther each. This [structure - a cyathium - ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyathium)is found in all the species of [*Euphorbia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphorbia), the genus to which the poinsettia and 2000 other species of spurge belong.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:300px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/cyathium.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/cyathium-300x263.jpg" alt="Cyathium [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Ies, Aelwyn]" /></a><figcaption>Cyathium of a different species of <em>Euphorbia</em>, but showing the single female flower, and here two males flowers [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Ies, Aelwyn]</figcaption></figure>

In one of the close relatives of the poinsettia - the crown-of-thorns - two coloured leaves surround just a single cyathium, making it a flower-squared: a bunch of tiny flowers masquerading as a single bloom:

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:300px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Euphorbia_milli.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Euphorbia_milli-300x200.jpg" alt="Euphorbia_milli [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Ezra Katz]" /></a><figcaption>Eight crown-of-thorns cyathia [CC-BY-SA-3.0 Ezra Katz] - as the author says on the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphorbia_milii#/media/File:Euphorbia_Milii_flowers.jpg">upload page on Wikipedia</a> "a close up of <em>Euphorbia milli</em> cyathia (not of the flowers!)"</figcaption></figure>

This means that the poinsettia 'flower' is really a flower-cubed: it is a set of tiny petal-less flowers grouped together into a cyathium, and then a bouquet of cyathia are grouped together and surrounded by coloured leaves. It is a flower reinvented - twice!

The cyathium is one of many re-invented wheels in the business of making flowers. All the 33 thousand species in the [daisy family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteraceae) have flowers-squared, as do all of the [arum lilies.](/2013/03/organism-of-the-week-10/) The flowering structures of [grasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaceae) are flowers-squared or cubed according to taste.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:300px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Silybum_marianum.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Silybum_marianum-300x225.jpg" alt="Silybum marianum [CC-BY-SA-4.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption>Thistles (like this <em>Silybum marianum</em>) and all the other daisy family have <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudanthium">flower-heads </a>that are composed of many very small flowers clustered together and - in this case - surrounded by spiky rather than 'pretty' leaves.</figcaption></figure>

Deeper down the rabbit-hole...

When I said the 'red leafy-looking things' on a poinsettia weren't *really* petals, I wasn't lying, but from an evolutionary point of view, that's all a petal actually is. Petals are a ring of leaves that became useful for attracting pollinators in some long-dead ancestor of the flowering plants.

Something similar applies to the male anthers and female carpels at the centre of a flower: these are also modified leaves, wrapped around the tissue that makes the pollen and egg-cells. So even a single real flower can be seen as a cluster of smaller male and female 'florets' (botanists call these [sporangia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sporangium)), themselves made of rolled leaves, and surrounded by yet more (prettily coloured) leaves. A flower is a leaf-squared, so a poinsettia 'flower' is a leaf-to-the-fourth-power.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:199px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Liriodendron_tulipifera_flower.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Liriodendron_tulipifera_flower-199x300.jpg" alt="Liriodendron tulipifera [CC-BY-SA-.4.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption>Tulip-tree magnolia (<em>Liriodendron tulipifera</em>) - some pretty leaves wrapped round some sexy leaves</figcaption></figure>

Oh, but we're not done yet.

What is a leaf? Well, if you hold up the leaf of most land plants to the light, you'll see a lot of veins. Depending on what you're looking at, these may be arranged in neat parallel lines, or in a tree-like branching network.

<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" style="max-width:267px"><a href="/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Cotinus_coggygria_autumn_leaf.jpg"><img loading="lazy" src="/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/Cotinus_coggygria_autumn_leaf-267x300.jpg" alt="Cotinus coggygria [CC-BY-SA-4.0 Steve Cook]" /></a><figcaption>A tree within a tree (<em>Cotinus coggygria</em>)</figcaption></figure>

The latter kind gives you a clue as to what a leaf really is. [A leaf is a much-branched but flattened stem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microphyll) with tissue stretched out across it like the webbing between the toes of a duck.

So if a leaf is a stem-squared, then a poinsettia 'flower' is a stem-to-the-fifth-power:  

- Start with a stem
- Group a bunch of stems together with some webbing to make a leaf
- Group a bunch of reproductive leaves together with some pretty leaves to make a flower
- Group a bunch of simplified flowers into a cyathium
- Group a bunch of cyathia together with some pretty leaves to make a poinsettia

  
Plants: what you get when you take [one good idea and run with it](/2013/05/organism-of-the-week-14/) for 500 million years.
